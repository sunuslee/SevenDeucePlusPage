PRACTICAL POKER GUIDE

How to settle a home poker game

Keep one clear record of buy-ins and cash-outs, then reconcile the table before anyone pays.

Agree on units and keep one record

Decide what each chip or point represents and who records the table. Enter each buy-in when it happens, including rebuys. Record players who leave early immediately instead of trying to reconstruct their numbers at the end of the night.

Calculate each player’s net result

Net result equals cash-out minus total buy-ins. A player who buys in for 1,000 and leaves with 1,400 is +400. Another who buys in for 1,000 and leaves with 600 is −400. Those results balance. A net result is an accounting entry, not evidence that a payment has been made.

Reconcile the whole table first

For a closed chip ledger with no deductions, total buy-ins should equal total cash-outs and player net results should sum to zero. If they do not, check missing rebuys, early departures, chip counts and inconsistent units. Account for any agreed expenses explicitly rather than hiding them in a player’s result.

Confirm, share and settle

Share the final scoreboard so everyone can check their numbers. Keep the final net result separate from payments already made. Players handle payments themselves; retain payment records where needed to avoid paying twice. Resolve an imbalance before deciding who owes the difference.
